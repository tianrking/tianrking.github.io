A capital, a boundary that continues to shape life on the Korean peninsula, and a port facing the sea are three good reasons to write this trip by place instead of by day. Incheon and the DMZ area have histories of their own; they are more than side trips from Seoul.

## Seoul: inside and outside the palace walls {/* #heritage */}

Gyeongbokgung is a useful starting point for the history of the capital. Built early in the Joseon dynasty, its central axis, gates and administrative spaces show how a court organized ceremony and government. Modern streets and public squares outside the walls tell a different story. The travel photographs can pin that story to a real walk without inferring that several palaces were visited. [Royal Palaces and Tombs Center: Gyeongbokgung and its history](https://royal.khs.go.kr/ENG/contents/E101010000.do)

## The DMZ: name the stops that can be verified {/* #dmz */}

The 1953 armistice left the Military Demarcation Line and Demilitarized Zone. Saying that a trip went to the DMZ area does not establish that it reached Panmunjom or the Joint Security Area, much less crossed the demarcation line. A public memorial area such as Imjingak, an observatory and the Third Tunnel offer quite different perspectives. The first can tell a story about separated families; an observatory confronts visitors with the difference between seeing a place and being able to reach it. Signs and exhibits will help identify the actual stop. [Korea Tourism Organization: DMZ tours and sites](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoHtmlView.do?vcontsId=139771)

## Incheon: opening a port and landing in wartime {/* #incheon */}

Incheon’s open-port district records the city’s late nineteenth-century opening to trade. Chinatown also belongs in the context of migration and port commerce. The 1950 Incheon Landing, by contrast, is an event of the Korean War with a memorial hall of its own. The two histories should not be conflated, and a trip to the city is not simply time spent at its airport. Street names or exhibition labels will show which part of Incheon belongs in the personal record. [Korea Tourism Organization: Open Port Area Street](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=351&vcontsId=69156) · [Incheon Landing Operation Memorial Hall](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=105436)
