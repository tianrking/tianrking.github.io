**Phnom Penh for state, court and modern memory; Siem Reap for contemporary life; Angkor for temples, cities and water.** These are complementary scales, not interchangeable sightseeing districts. Linking a sculpture in a museum with a lintel in the archaeological park and a living city makes history more than a sequence of rulers and dates.

Phnom Penh, Siem Reap and Angkor are confirmed visited areas. This article organises them into revisable travel notes and a **10-day, nine-night return-trip framework**. The day order is not the original travel log; named sites are reading and route suggestions, not assertions that every museum was visited. Unconfirmed hotels, costs, dates and personal experiences are not invented.

**Public information checked: 13 November 2026.** The main route arrives at Phnom Penh KTI and departs Siem Reap SAI. A KTI-return alternative appears below. Use a daytime through-bus between cities; Thai border crossings, unmanaged remote ruins and a sleepless arrival straight into an intensive sunrise tour are not part of the plan.

## Ten days and nine nights {/* #route */}

Two bases are sufficient: three consecutive nights in Phnom Penh and six in Siem Reap. Visit the park on D5, D6, D8 and D9, with a restorative city/museum day on D7. Angkor Wat and Angkor Thom each receive their own focus rather than fitting every celebrated temple into a single sunrise circuit.

| Day | Focus | Sleep |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Arrive KTI, settle in; riverside or market only if early | Phnom Penh |
| D2 | Royal Palace/Silver Pagoda, National Museum and riverside urban space | Phnom Penh |
| D3 | Tuol Sleng/S-21 and Choeung Ek; keep emotional space afterward | Phnom Penh |
| D4 | Daytime through-bus to Siem Reap; check-in and park preparation | Siem Reap |
| D5 | Park day 1: Angkor Wat in depth and Ta Prohm | Siem Reap |
| D6 | Park day 2: Angkor Thom gate, Bayon, Baphuon and royal terraces | Siem Reap |

**This full reading route uses four park days.** The official current options are US$37 for one day, US$62 for three days within seven days and US$72 for seven days within 30 days. A seven-day pass does not require seven visits; it accommodates this four-day itinerary. Do not budget a three-day pass while listing four separate entry days. ## The names describe different places {/* #heritage */}

**Angkor** is the wider heritage landscape of successive cities, temples, roads, water works and living settlements. **Angkor Wat** is one temple. **Angkor Thom** is a walled capital containing Bayon and other monuments. **Siem Reap** is the usual accommodation and food base. UNESCO describes a roughly 400-square-kilometre archaeological landscape; “how long for one temple?” cannot answer how long for the whole area. [UNESCO: Angkor](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/668/)

| Place | Question to carry | Look beyond |
|---|---|---|
| Angkor Wat | How do causeway, enclosure, gallery and elevated levels organise arrival and viewing? | Five towers and reflections: read one relief sequence closely |
| Angkor Thom/Bayon | How do city entrance, central temple, royal space and public terraces connect? | Tower faces: notice boats, warfare, trade and daily scenes |
| Ta Prohm | How are vegetation, stone stability, supports and visitor routes managed together? | Roots over masonry: conservation is not simply damage to an imagined untouched ruin |
| Preah Khan and water sites | How do long axes, thresholds, institutions and water environments relate? | A photogenic corridor: read the whole before a detail |
| Banteay Srei | What changes with material, carving density and architectural scale? | A small footprint is not a three-minute visit |

Read pose, attributes, vehicle and scene before identifying a deity. Not every female figure is the same kind of celestial dancer, and not every tower face has one simple interpretation. Record an uncertain image and location for later comparison rather than fabricating an explanation. A qualified interpreting guide is most valuable on D5 or D6; driving and historical interpretation are different jobs.

Compare the total cost of **KTI in / SAI out** with a KTI return before booking nine nights. Buy the actual dated daytime bus and use its ticketed terminals. ## The daily reading route {/* #days */}

### D1 | Phnom Penh arrival: use KTI, not the former PNH {/* #d1 */}

Navigate to [Techo International Airport KTI](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Techo+International+Airport+KTI+Cambodia). Air Cambodia announced the transfer of its Phnom Penh services from the former PNH to KTI on 9 September 2025. Old airport-bus starting points and city-transfer assumptions cannot simply be reused. [Official airport transition notice](https://www.aircambodia.com/en/offer/important-notice)

Compare accommodation around the National Museum/Royal Palace and accessible riverside streets with established dining/accommodation areas such as BKK1. The first favours D2 walking; the second may suit a quieter base but does not remove daily vehicle needs. Check air-conditioning, reception, recent reviews and the real street entrance. Immigration, baggage, cash and city access need separate allowances; landing time is not a palace appointment.

After an early arrival, walk Sisowath Quay and read the relationship between river and city. Add at most one of Wat Phnom or Central Market. After a late arrival, eat and rest. Secure phones and bags in vehicles, keep cash separate and avoid leaning into traffic for photographs.

### D2 | Palace and National Museum: a vocabulary for the later temples {/* #d2 */}

Allow 2–3 morning hours for the [Royal Palace/Silver Pagoda](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Royal+Palace+Phnom+Penh+Cambodia), confirming the day’s ticketing, breaks and royal events. This is a modern courtly, religious and national space, not a direct model of the original Angkorian palace. Look at roofs, courtyards, axes, guardian figures and the boundary between public and restricted space. Follow dress and photography requirements.

After lunch, give the [National Museum of Cambodia](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=National+Museum+of+Cambodia+Phnom+Penh) 2–3 hours. Compare sculptural material, attributes and periods rather than memorising every king. Notice headdresses, proportion, gestures and how fragments are identified. The museum currently publishes daily 08:00–17:00 opening, last ticket 16:30 and US$10 admission for foreign adults aged 18+. Gallery photography is prohibited; courtyard/exterior rules differ. [Official admission information](https://nmc.gov.kh/index.php/pages/museum-admission)

Keep an evening riverside walk optional. Comparing museum objects with living devotional practice can be more useful than adding another distant attraction. Do not put palace, museum and both major memorials into one day. If royal access closes, prioritise the museum and rearrange available palace time without consuming D3’s whole memorial visit.

### D3 | Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek: evidence, lives and remembrance {/* #d3 */}

Give [Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum/S-21](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tuol+Sleng+Genocide+Museum+Phnom+Penh) 2.5–3 hours with official audio or suitable interpretation. Read archives, rooms and victims’ lives, not just instruments of violence. Continue to [Choeung Ek](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Choeung+Ek+Genocidal+Center+Cambodia) only if you have the capacity. Confirm legitimate road transport, waiting and return timing; these sites are not a short walk apart. [Museum visitor/contact information](https://tuolsleng.gov.kh/en/contact/)

Together with former M-13, they became the 2025 World Heritage property “Cambodian Memorial Sites: From centres of repression to places of peace and reflection”. Their purpose is evidence, remembrance and education, not a ranking of shocking attractions. M-13 has different visitor-access conditions and is not an automatic third excursion. [UNESCO property and access context](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1748)

| Window | Plan | Protect |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Tuol Sleng, interpretation and archives | More than a quick photograph visit |
| Midday | Quiet meal and pause | A real decision about continuing |
| Afternoon | Choeung Ek and return | Legitimate transport, access and emotional space |
| Evening | Rest and packing | No compulsory entertainment afterward |

Follow rules for remains, objects and images. Do not pose at memorials, interrupt mourning or ask survivors to reproduce private trauma. Stopping after one thoughtful visit is acceptable; emotional overload is not a travel target to complete.

### D4 | A daytime journey to Siem Reap {/* #d4 */}

Take a confirmed dated daytime through-bus. Giant Ibis describes roughly six hours, but local access, check-in, stops, traffic and the arrival-to-hotel transfer are extra. Verify the actual vehicle and terminals; its night-bus descriptions of bunks, toilets and pickup do not automatically apply to a daytime service. [Operator schedule](https://online.giantibis.com/schedule) · [Official route](https://online.giantibis.com/routes/phnom-penh-to-siem-reap)

After arrival, check in, eat and hydrate. Stay six nights around the Old Market/Wat Bo area but away from Pub Street speakers, or compare quieter Taphul surroundings. There is no need to move hotels around different temple gates. Confirm breakfast, early takeaway options, laundry and reception; a sunrise plan should not rely on an empty stomach and no sleep.

Confirm the driver/guide, four routes and pass before D5. Select activation dates carefully. Do not rush a sunset park entry after the bus: it can consume an entry day without a proper visit. Protect four useful archaeological days rather than adding a tired arrival photograph.

### D5 | Angkor Wat and Ta Prohm: monument and conservation landscape {/* #d5 */}

Give [Angkor Wat](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Angkor+Wat+Cambodia) 3–4 hours. Begin with moat, causeway and successive thresholds, then galleries and towers, then selected reliefs. Sunrise is optional, not necessary to understand the building. If chosen, confirm pickup, breakfast, lighting and admission, and still tour the monument afterward. A reflection photograph is not the whole visit.

Use a sequence such as the Churning of the Ocean of Milk to understand narrative, figures, viewing order and repair. Access to Bakan may be affected by religious days, capacity, weather or works; an unavailable upper level does not invalidate reading the galleries and layout. WMF’s projects include conservation of this relief gallery, bringing art and preservation into one visit. [WMF’s Angkor projects](https://www.wmf.org/monuments/angkor-archaeological-park)

After a midday break, allow 1.5–2 hours at [Ta Prohm](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ta+Prohm+Temple+Angkor+Cambodia). Look at roots and walls, but also supports, reassembled stone and visitor routes. Shade does not prevent slippery footing. Do not add Bayon, Banteay Srei and a sunset hill to this same day: the two main visits already deserve sustained attention.

### D6 | Angkor Thom: gate, Bayon and royal/public space {/* #d6 */}

Enter by the [South Gate](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=South+Gate+of+Angkor+Thom+Cambodia), reading moat, gateway and road before [Bayon](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bayon+Temple+Angkor+Thom+Cambodia). Stay off the vehicle lane while photographing. Give Bayon at least 1.5–2 hours and look beyond faces to reliefs of boats, military activity, exchange and daily life. Follow actual level-access restrictions; an upper-level climb is not guaranteed.

Continue according to energy to Baphuon, permitted Royal Palace/Phimeanakas areas and the Elephant and Leper King terraces. Sharing a walled city does not make each a ten-minute stop. Reduce climbing if heat, stairs or restoration slow progress. Agree vehicle meeting points in advance rather than assuming the driver will find a different exit.

Carry an urban plan as well as temple photographs: gate, centre, palace and terraces establish the capital-city scale missing from a stand-alone monument visit. Return early enough to rest. Preah Khan belongs to D8; four entry days are useful precisely because every major site need not be forced into one exhausting circuit.

### D7 | Siem Reap: a museum after the first temples {/* #d7 */}

Spend 2–3 morning hours at [Angkor National Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Angkor+National+Museum+Siem+Reap), bringing questions from the previous two days. This is not Phnom Penh’s National Museum and its ticket is separate from the park pass. Galleries on civilisation, belief, kings, Angkor Wat and Angkor Thom help organise images and architectural observations after seeing them in place. [Museum official site](https://angkornationalmuseum.com/)

Walk Wat Bo, riverside and Old Market surroundings afterward for contemporary religious and everyday life. For a modern-war aftermath perspective, an optional one-hour **APOPO Visitor Center** tour explains humanitarian mine detection; it is not a tourist outing into a minefield. The official current information lists 08:30–17:00 daily, last tour 16:30, US$10, with online booking recommended. Recheck your date. [APOPO official visitor information](https://apopo.org/support-us/apopo-visitor-center/)

For contemporary arts, check actual [Phare Cambodian Circus](https://pharecircus.org/) performances and ticket categories. Museum, APOPO, craft class and performance are not all compulsory purchases. This day restores attention and energy; staying outside the park does not “waste a ticket” when the validity window permits nonconsecutive visits.

### D8 | Preah Khan and the water landscape {/* #d8 */}

Follow **[Preah Khan](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Preah+Khan+Temple+Angkor+Siem+Reap) → [Neak Pean](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Neak+Pean+Angkor+Cambodia) → [Ta Som](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ta+Som+Temple+Angkor)**. Include Angkor/Siem Reap when navigating: remote Preah Khan of Kampong Svay is a different place, not this circuit’s temple.

Give Preah Khan 1.5–2 hours for axes, doorways, courtyards and changing spatial scales, not just a straight-corridor photograph. Interpretation of stone stability, vegetation and craft training reveals a contemporary conservation site as well as an old institution. WMF’s visitor interpretation work offers this additional reading. [WMF on Preah Khan interpretation](https://www.wmf.org/news/new-visitor-experience-preah-khan)

At Neak Pean, read the relationship between water, approach and sacred space; seasonal water and access affect what you see. Allow around 45–60 minutes for Ta Som. Add East Mebon only if energy remains, comparing its platform with the earlier water environment. Rain or heat is a reason to finish after the main three, not to force another sunset at Pre Rup. Agree rest, waiting and exit pickup in advance.

### D9 | Banteay Srei: smaller scale, slower looking {/* #d9 */}

Travel to [Banteay Srei](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Banteay+Srei+Temple+Siem+Reap+Cambodia) with the longer transfer included in your quote. Allow 1.5–2 hours on site. The tourist name does not mean a simple palace where women lived: focus on the tenth-century temple, red sandstone, lintels and pediments. Small-scale carving does not justify crossing a protective boundary; careful looking and a suitable lens are enough. [Angkor Enterprise’s temple introduction](https://www.angkorenterprise.gov.kh/en/explore/685a0d609b66c0d6db9be3e9)

Consider Banteay Samre on the return or go directly to town for lunch and rest. Do not chain Kbal Spean, Beng Mealea, Koh Ker and Tonle Sap onto this day: they are not consecutive nearby stops automatically covered by one “outer circuit” purchase. Transport, permission and ticketing require separate checks. One focused comparison between monumental D5 and detailed D9 is the point.

Organise photographs with verified temple names, locations and questions. Leave uncertain deity identities, uses and local stories open rather than turning an unverified explanation into historical fact. Confirm SAI transport and baggage that evening. This is the fourth park day and belongs in the seven-day-pass budget, not an undisclosed extra day-ticket purchase.

### D10 | SAI departure: allow the out-of-town transfer {/* #d10 */}

Use [Siem Reap–Angkor International Airport SAI](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Siem+Reap+Angkor+International+Airport+SAI), not former REP. Plan to reach the airport about three hours before an international flight, adding hotel-to-stop access, waiting, road travel and current conditions. If the bus cannot serve an early departure, reserve legitimate transport beforehand.

The airport’s official bus information explicitly excludes hotel pickup/drop-off. Its current city-stop reference is on 7 Makara Road in front of ACE School; recheck stop, schedule and fare rather than treating older duty-free-store instructions as reliable. A map pin is not a dated bus booking. [Official SAI ground transport](https://english.sai-airport.com/jcjtjb/38836.jhtml)

No Banteay Srei, lake boat or distant temple is scheduled this day. A Phnom Penh departure requires the alternative below, not squeezing a nominal six-hour bus into airport day. Read after-midnight departure dates and final hotel checkout correctly; handle shopping after reaching the correct terminal.

A driver handles road travel and meetings; a licensed interpreting guide supplies supported historical explanation. One qualified person may do both, but speaking English is not proof of guiding qualification. Quote your actual party size. Do not build the trip around night buses just to remove a room. With only one main intercity move, a day bus preserves landscape and sleep. If you independently choose an overnight service, check bunk sharing, pickup, baggage and early storage; free early check-in and an immediate sunrise tour are not assumed.

## Landscape, seasons and adjustments {/* #seasonal */}

Nature is already present in moats, reservoirs, woodland, fields and village landscapes. D8’s water context and the D9 countryside provide variety without automatically adding a long excursion. A Tonle Sap trip should replace half a day or a day, not be appended casually. Check seasonal water, legitimate boats, lifejackets, complete charges and respectful community access; do not turn residents or donation pressure into a spectacle.

Start early and break at midday in heat; wet-season roads, thunderstorms and water levels need attention. Dry season is not a no-rain guarantee. One optional sunrise is sufficient; accept clouds rather than repeatedly losing sleep for the same reflection. D7 can move inside the pass window if the total park-day count and departure protection remain intact.

| Change | Adjustment | Protect |
|---|---|---|
| Palace closure | Museum first; use a later genuine palace opening | D3 memorial time |
| Rain at sunrise | A normal early visit to Angkor Wat | More than a reflection photo |
| Closed upper level/walkway | Open reliefs, areas or a nearby same-route stop | No climbing barriers or loose masonry |
| Temple fatigue | Remove one park day; select a three-day pass if not bought yet | A restorative city day |
| Flights require KTI return | D9 daytime bus to Phnom Penh, overnight; D10 KTI departure | Three park days, no Banteay Srei day; nights become Phnom Penh 3 + Siem Reap 5 + Phnom Penh 1 |

## Entry, safety and visiting ethics {/* #risk */}

The Chinese-passport visa-waiver window was **15 June–15 October 2026**, a limited arrangement, not evidence of waiver eligibility in November, December or a later year. The official eVisa page currently lists Tourist Visa T at US$30. Check eligibility, stay, processing and arrival port for your actual date. Submit free e-Arrival within seven days before arrival; it is not a substitute for a visa. [Official eVisa](https://www.evisa.gov.kh/) · [Immigration: free e-Arrival](https://immigration.gov.kh/post/1859) · [Declaration portal](https://arrival.gov.kh/)

The Thai border is not a nearby add-on. Australia’s current advice warns against travel within 50 km of the border and reports closed land crossings and explosive-remnant risks. That is not a statement that all Cambodia is inaccessible, nor permission to ignore changes on the main route. Recheck your own consular advice, insurance, local notices and roads before departure; no border-temple detour is assumed. [Current regional travel advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/cambodia)

Stay on permitted archaeological routes. Do not touch carvings, climb restoration stones, collect unexploded objects or buy antiquities of unclear origin. Memorials are not posing locations. Dress respectfully in temples, obtain permission for close portraits and do not join orphanage tourism. Check drivers, protect phones from roadside snatching and keep document/cash copies separate. Cheap travel is not a reason to use unlicensed night transport.
